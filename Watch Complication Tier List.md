### **Tier A: High-Complication**

### *These represent the pinnacle of watchmaking. They require hundreds of tiny components, immense assembly time.*

* **Minute Repeater:** Chimes the time down to the minute using tiny internal hammers and gongs.  
* **Tourbillon:** A rotating cage that houses the escapement to counter gravity. *(Note: While technically an escapement and not a "complication," the industry universally ranks it as a top-tier feature).*  
* **Perpetual Calendar:** Tracks the day, date, month, and moonphase, automatically adjusting for leap years until the year 2100\.  
* **Split-Seconds Chronograph / Rattrapante:** Two superimposed chronograph hands that allow you to time two separate events simultaneously.  
* **Grande / Petite Sonnerie:** Automatically chimes the hours and quarters as they pass, like a grandfather clock on the wrist.  
* **Celestial / Sky Chart:** A rotating map of the night sky exactly as it appears from a specific location on Earth.

---

### **Tier B: Mid-Complication** 

### *These are highly respected, technically impressive features that are practical for daily or travel wear.* 

* **Annual Calendar:** Tracks the day, date, and month, automatically adjusting for 30- and 31-day months. It only needs one manual adjustment per year (at the end of February).  
* **Retrograde:** A hand that moves along an arc to display a measurement (like the date or seconds) and instantly snaps back to zero when it reaches the end.  
* **Standard Chronograph:** A standard stopwatch function (e.g., Rolex Daytona, Omega Speedmaster).  
* **Flyback Chronograph:** Allows you to reset and restart the chronograph instantly with a single push of a button, without needing to stop it first.  
* **World Time:** Displays the time in all 24 major global time zones simultaneously on the dial (e.g., Patek Philippe 5230).  
* **GMT / Dual Time:** A second hour hand (often 24-hour) that tracks a second time zone (e.g., Rolex GMT-Master II).  
* **Moonphase:** Tracks the 29.5-day lunar cycle.

### **Tier C: Low-Complication / Basic**

### *The foundational elements of a watch. These prioritize legibility, reliability, and everyday utility.*

* **Three-Hand / Center Seconds:** Hours, minutes, and a central sweeping seconds hand.  
* **Small Seconds:** Hours and minutes in the center, with the seconds ticking in a smaller sub-dial (often at 6 o'clock).  
* **Two-Hand:** Hours and minutes only, with no seconds hand (very common in ultra-thin classic dress watches like the Cartier Tank or Patek Calatrava).  
* **Date Window:** A simple numerical date display that must be manually advanced after any month with fewer than 31 days.  
* **Day-Date:** Displays both the day of the week and the date (e.g., Rolex Day-Date).  
* **Power Reserve Indicator:** A gauge showing how much tension is left in the mainspring before the watch stops ticking.

---

**Tier scale: A = most complicated (top), B = mid, C = basic (entry).**
